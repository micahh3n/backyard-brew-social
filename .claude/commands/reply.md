Draft a Backyard Brew reply to this: $ARGUMENTS

## Do this

1. Read the `backyard-brew-brand` skill and `references/reply-rules.md`
   before drafting a single word.
2. **Triage it first.** Type A genuine issue, Type B false or fabricated, or
   Type C positive. Say which, in one line, before the draft.
3. If the facts are unclear, ask one question rather than writing a confident
   correction that might be wrong.

## If he included what he actually wants to say

He may paste the review along with his own unfiltered reaction. That is the
point of this command, and it is the most useful thing he can give you.

**Take it seriously as input, never as the draft.** Find the legitimate point
inside it, which there almost always is, and write the version that makes that
point land harder than the angry one would have. Calm and factual beats heated
in front of strangers, every time.

**Do not comment on his tone.** He knows. That is why he is here. Just hand
back the better version.

## Deliver

**One reply, ready to paste.** Not three to choose from. Commit to one, unless
the situation is genuinely borderline, in which case give a softer and a
firmer version and say which you would send.

Then, one line each:
- Which type it is, and whether to report it to Google before responding
- Anything he should verify before it goes public

## Remember

The reply is written for the next person reading it, not for the reviewer. The
reviewer already decided. The people choosing where to go Friday have not.

Four sentences is usually the maximum on a negative review. Length reads as
guilt.

Never in public: calling anyone a liar or fake, mentioning that it was
reported, speculating about motives, sarcasm, or a second reply to the same
person.

If the review genuinely does not deserve a response, say so. A reply pushes it
up the page.
