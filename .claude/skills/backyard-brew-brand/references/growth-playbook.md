# Growth Playbook

The strategy for growing Backyard Brew's online presence and converting it
into people walking through the door. Read this with `operations-reality.md`
before answering anything about growth, marketing, SEO, or new events.

The goal is never reach for its own sake. Every recommendation has to end in
someone driving to 3640 Sand Acres Dr.

---

## Priority order

When asked "what should we do next," work down this list. Do not suggest
something from tier 3 while tier 1 is still broken.

**Tier 1: highest return, currently neglected**
1. **Google Business Profile.** Claimed but nobody maintains it. This is the
   single biggest lever available and it is free. Full punch list below.
2. **Review volume.** More reviews raises both the map ranking and the star
   average. Already a stated priority.

**Tier 2: fix what is underperforming**
3. **Tuesday and Wednesday reach.** Good events, wrong audience seeing them.
4. **Facebook group reposting.** Free distribution the bar already has access
   to and does not fully use.

**Tier 3: build new**
5. **Saturday daytime and Sunday concepts.** Two empty windows.
6. **Website and AI search visibility.**

## 1. Google Business Profile

For a destination business in a suburb, the map pack beats everything else.
Someone searching "things to do in Green Bay," "disc golf near me," or "bars
with outdoor space Green Bay" decides from the map result, not the website.

### Baseline, from a real audit (2026-08-04 — re-check periodically, don't assume it's still current forever)

**4.7 stars, 79 reviews.** Primary category is **Bar and nothing else**.
Hours are correct all 7 days on Google itself (Sunday 11am-7pm is right
here — the problem is everywhere else, see below). Price range $10-20.
Only 3 attributes on (wheelchair entrance, dine-in, takeout). **"Delivery"
shows as enabled, which is wrong — the bar doesn't deliver.** **"Backyard
Breakfast" is still sitting in the Menu highlights**, left over from before
breakfast was discontinued (2026-07-26) — this is the one a customer could
actually see and be wrong about. Google's own review keyword chips: disc
golf mentioned 15 times, bingo 11, local beer 8, pinball 2.

**Category order, locked (owner's call, 2026-08-10): Bar stays primary,
permanently. The bar is where the money is made.** Disc golf course goes in
as the top-priority secondary category, not a candidate to ever replace
Bar as primary — don't suggest flipping primary to Disc golf course, no
matter how secondary placement performs. This overrides the earlier idea of
revisiting primary after 30 days; that's off the table.

The disc-golf angle is still real and worth chasing as a secondary: ranked
as a Bar, competing against Badger State (610 reviews), Bark & Brew (342),
Oakley's (248) is close to unwinnable. Ranked as a disc golf course, the
field is Pinecrest (59 reviews) and Baird Creek (45) — 79 reviews at 4.7
stars would win that bracket outright as a secondary placement, which is
exactly why it's worth having even though Bar stays primary.

**Category setup: Bar (primary) → Disc golf course (top secondary) → Beer
garden → Hiking area → Live music bar → Event venue** (the last four in
whatever order GBP allows, disc golf specifically first among the
secondaries since it's the one with a real bracket-winning case behind it).

**Punch list, in order — items marked (2026-08-04) are the specific,
already-found fixes from that audit, not hypothetical checks:**

- [ ] **Hours are correct**, including Sunday. Already true on Google itself

      the directory list below.
- [ ] **Delete "Backyard Breakfast" from Menu highlights** (2026-08-04) —
      breakfast was discontinued 2026-07-26 and this is the one place it's
      still visibly wrong to a customer.
- [ ] **Check whether "Delivery" is genuinely enabled and turn it off**
      (2026-08-04) — the bar doesn't deliver; a customer expecting delivery

- [ ] **Special hours** set for holidays before they arrive.
- [ ] **Categories: Bar stays primary, permanently.** Add Disc golf course as

- [ ] **Attributes** — only 3 are on today (2026-08-04). Add outdoor seating,

- [ ] **Photos, weekly.** Profiles that get fresh photos outrank profiles that

## 2. Reviews

More reviews and a high average, in that order. Volume moves the map ranking;
average protects conversion once someone is looking.

**Getting more:**
- Ask in person at the right moment. Right after someone wins Bingo, wins the
  pool tournament, or finishes their first round on the course. Not when
  handing them a bill.
- A QR code at the bar and on the first tee that opens the Google review form
  directly. Not the profile, the review form.
- Event winners are the warmest possible audience. Ask them.
- Never offer anything in exchange for a review. It violates Google's terms
  and can get the profile penalized.

**Handling bad ones:** see `reply-rules.md`. The short version is report
first, respond second, and never respond angry.

## 3. Tuesday and Wednesday

Both events are good. The people who would love them do not know they exist.
Fix distribution, not the events.

**Tuesday, Pickleball Open Play:**
- Local pickleball Facebook groups and club pages. Pickleball players are
  organized online and travel for open play.
- Public court bulletin boards in the Green Bay area.
- Post results, not just announcements. "Last night's winners" gives the
  regulars something to share and shows newcomers the format is real.
- The 6:40pm cutoff is a genuine urgency hook. Use it.

**Wednesday, Tacos + Poker Club:**
- Local poker and card-game groups.
- Lead with tacos for the general audience and poker for the poker audience.
  Same night, two different pitches, two different places to post it.
- Free to play with a beer purchase is a strong offer that gets buried. Say
  it earlier.

**For both:** the aim is a repeatable core group. Ten people who come every
week beats forty who came once.

## 4. Facebook groups

Local groups are the highest-value free distribution the bar has. Most ban
business self-promotion, which is why reposting happens from personal
accounts as a person sharing something local rather than a business
advertising.

Rules and the group-matching table live in the printed daily sheet,
`playbook/3-EVERY-DAY.md`. The short version:

- Personal accounts, not the business page.
- Match the post to the group. A poker post goes to card and game groups, not
  the neighborhood page.
- Write a human line above the share. Never paste the marketing caption raw.
- Space them out. Do not blast eight groups inside one minute.
- Follow each group's rules. Getting banned costs more than any single post
  gains.

## 5. Website and AI search

`backyard-brew.com` runs on a custom WordPress theme, actively maintained
(v6.9.6 as of 2026-08-09). **The old Sunday-closed bug on the website itself
is fixed** (shipped in v6.3, 2026-08-04, and confirmed still correct through
several later updates) — don't send anyone chasing that on the site anymore.
**The Sunday-hours problem now lives on the third-party listings instead**
(Yelp, Restaurantji, UDisc) — see the Google Business Profile section above
for the specific ones and what's wrong on each.

**Traditional SEO priorities:**
- LocalBusiness schema with the exact NAP (name, address, phone), hours, and
  geo coordinates. Add Event schema for the recurring weekly events.
- NAP identical everywhere: Google, Facebook, the site, Apple Maps, Yelp, any
  directory. Inconsistency splits ranking signals.
- A real page per recurring event rather than one events page. "Bingo night
  Green Bay" and "karaoke Green Bay" are searches people actually make.
- Pages for the searches that describe what the bar is: disc golf course,
  hiking trails, Wisconsin-only taproom.

**AI search (GEO):** people increasingly ask ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and
Perplexity for "unique things to do in Green Bay." These systems quote
specific, factual, self-contained sentences. Write pages that state facts
plainly: what it is, where, hours, cost, how long the course takes, what makes
it different. Vague marketing prose does not get cited. A specific, quotable
claim like "an almost entirely Wisconsin-made tap list" is exactly the kind of
sentence these systems pick up — **but don't publish the literal "100%
Wisconsin, zero outside brands" version as a fact-checkable statement.**
There's a small, real Domestics exception (Miller Lite, Corona Extra — see
the parent skill), and an AI system or a customer taking that claim at face
value against the actual tap list is exactly the failure mode GEO content
is supposed to avoid. Scope the claim ("nearly every beer on tap is
Wisconsin-made") instead of overstating it.

For an actual audit rather than a plan, the `claude-seo` skills are installed
and can run a real technical and local audit against the live site.

## 6. Event concepts for the two dead windows

Ranked. Each fits the property, the Wisconsin-only rule, the small crew, and
the existing schedule. None duplicate a current event.

Before proposing anything not on this list, re-read the weekly table in
`operations-reality.md` and the monthly events in the parent skill.

### Saturday, 11am to roughly 4pm

**1. Saturday Doubles on the course (strongest)**
Random-draw doubles disc golf, mid-morning tee time, $5 course fee plus a
small entry, winners take tap credit. Disc golf leagues run themselves once
the format is set, players stay to drink afterward, and it directly sells
$150 course memberships. Lowest staff load of anything here: someone collects
entries and posts the pairings. Disc golfers already drive to courses on
Saturday mornings, so this competes for an audience that is out and looking.

**2. Trail and Tap**
A self-guided 3.5-mile loop with a discounted pour at the finish. Free to
enter, monetized at the bar. Works for hikers, families, and dogs, none of
whom currently have a reason to show up before evening. Nearly zero staff
cost, and it markets the trails, which are the least-known part of the
property.

**3. Blind-draw bags tournament**
Cornhole, indoors or out, early afternoon. Wisconsin to its core, needs no new
equipment, and pairs with the existing bags setup. Weaker than the first two
because it does not use the outdoor property, but it works in bad weather when
the other two do not.

**4. Beginner disc golf clinic**
Monthly rather than weekly. Teaches people who have never thrown, loaner discs
provided, parents drink while kids learn. Converts non-players into course
regulars, which is the pipeline for memberships. Needs one staff member who
can teach, so it costs more than the others.

### Sunday, 11am to 7pm

**DECIDED and BUILT 2026-08-10: Packers Sundays.** Owner's call. This is the
concept — the options below are kept for context on why, not as still-open
alternatives. Live in `recurring_events.csv` (a seasonal Sunday row,
Sept-Jan only) and the `/sunday` command. **Kickoff time is deliberately
left open** — it changes weekly with the NFL schedule, so the owner
confirms the real time each week before it goes in a post rather than
Claude guessing or reusing last week's.

**1. Packers Sundays (seasonal, September to January) — chosen**
In Green Bay this is not optional, it is the whole day. Frame it as a backyard
tailgate rather than a sports bar: a round on the course before kickoff, the
game outside, Wisconsin taps, tailgate food. It fills the exact window that is
empty and it uses the one cultural fact that guarantees a crowd here. The
brand is not a sports bar, and the framing matters, but Packers references are
explicitly welcome. **This alone likely fixes Sunday for five months.**

**2. Sunday Funday league finals or a glow round**
Give Saturday's doubles league a Sunday championship, or run a late-afternoon
glow round as the light drops. Gives the disc golf community a second reason
to come out and creates a natural rivalry story to post about.

**3. Family Sunday**
Kids eat cheap or free with an adult, family disc golf rates, dogs welcome.
Sunday afternoon is when families look for somewhere to go, and the property
suits them better than any bar in the area. Costs margin on food, so check the
numbers before committing.

**4. Winter: snowshoe Sundays (seasonal, replaces the above)**
The trails support snowshoeing and almost nothing else in the area does.
Rentals or bring-your-own, warm drinks after. Turns the slowest season into a
differentiator instead of a dead zone.

### Before recommending any of these

Ask what the crew can realistically staff, and check whether it has been tried
already. This list was written 2026-07-27 and none of it had been attempted at
that point.

## Measuring

Vanity metrics do not matter here. Track:

- Google Business Profile views, searches, and direction requests. Direction
  requests are the closest thing to a foot-traffic counter.
- Review count and average, monthly.
- Headcount on Tuesday and Wednesday specifically, since those are the fix.
- Whether the new Saturday or Sunday concept drew anyone at all in week one,
  and whether it grew by week four.

Ask the owner for the actual numbers rather than guessing. If a push has run
four weeks with no movement in door count, change the approach instead of
posting harder.
