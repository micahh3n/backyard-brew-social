# Operations Reality

How the business actually runs, where it is weak, who works there, and what
the owner is trying to achieve. Read this before answering any question about
what the bar should DO (new events, growth ideas, staffing, priorities), not
just questions about how it should SOUND.

Recorded from the owner's son 2026-07-27, right before he left for college.
Anything marked "as of 2026-07-27" is a snapshot and may have changed. When a
detail here is load-bearing for a recommendation and it is more than a few
months old, ask before assuming it still holds.

### The Tuesday/Wednesday diagnosis

The owner's son was specific about this: the events themselves are good. Both
Tuesday and Wednesday could be amazing nights. The gap is reach, not quality.
The right people are not hearing about them.

That makes Tuesday and Wednesday a **targeting problem, not a programming
problem.** Do not suggest replacing or redesigning Pickleball Open Play or
Tacos + Poker Club. Suggest ways to put them in front of pickleball players
and poker players specifically: local pickleball groups and clubs, poker
communities, the Facebook groups where those people already talk, players at
nearby courts. See `growth-playbook.md`.

### The real gaps

1. **Saturday 11am to roughly 4pm.** The doors are open and the property is
   the best version of itself in daylight, and almost nobody is there. Still
   fully open.
2. **Sunday, 11am-noon and 4pm-7pm.** Backyard Market & Brews now fills
   noon-4pm year-round (moved from Thursday 2026-09-13), and Sept-Jan the
   market also carries the Sunday football crowd. The edges of the day —
   before the market opens and after it closes — are what's left open, a
   much smaller gap than before.

Saturday is where inventing something new is the strongest answer. When
someone asks for event ideas, assume they mean Saturday daytime unless they
say otherwise, or the Sunday edges specifically.

## Staff (as of 2026-07-27, actively changing)

Small crew. Plan around it.

- **The owner (dad)**: runs the business. After his son leaves he is also
  the default for social media, reviews, and comments.
- **The owner's son**: left for college late August 2026. Previously handled
  all social media posting, all graphics, and all review and comment
  responses. This entire playbook exists to replace him.
- **The owner's wife (mom)**: helps sometimes, not every shift.
- **At least three other employees**, as of this snapshot (headcount may have
  grown since — more hiring was planned):
  - One is brand new and still training.
  - One is excellent, well liked, and genuinely good with social media. The
    most realistic person to help with content.
  - One is a part-timer who is good at her job and pleasant, and who **does
    not want to be involved in the bar's social media.** Respect this. Never
    build a plan that depends on her being on camera or posting.
- **The owner's younger daughter**: occasional help. **Cooking and cleaning
  only. Not permitted to bartend.** Never suggest otherwise.
- **More hiring is planned** after the son leaves.

### How to handle staffing in recommendations

The owner asked for guidelines he can delegate from, not job descriptions or
quotas assigned to named people. Write daily and weekly routines as **things
that should happen**, and let him decide who does each one. Do not invent a
"social media coordinator" role or hand anyone a required posting count.

Any event or growth idea has to be runnable by a handful of people who are
already bartending. If an idea needs a dedicated staffer to run it, say so
plainly so the owner can price that in.

## Goals

Stated directly by the owner's son:

- Every day busy. Not just the strong nights.
- Grow daily revenue and keep profit ahead of costs.
- Profit funds three things, in this order of intent: **hire more people**,
  **improve the building**, and **invest in new things that make the
  experience better.**
- Grow online presence so it converts into people physically walking in. Reach
  that does not turn into foot traffic is not the goal.

No acute cash crisis as of this snapshot. Some days simply bring in less than
they should. Treat weak days as the opportunity, not as an emergency.

## Customers and complaints

Complaints are rare. When a real one comes in, it gets fixed.

The small number of people who complain fall into two groups:

1. **Genuine issue.** Gets resolved directly, and the underlying problem gets
   fixed. Normal business.
2. **Pre-existing bias.** People who dislike the bar for reasons unconnected
   to anything that happened to them, some of whom have never been inside.

Telling these two apart is the first step in any response. See
`reply-rules.md`.

## Constraints to respect in any recommendation

- **Wisconsin-only beverages, with one documented exception:** the small
  Domestics category (Miller Lite, Corona Extra). See the parent skill's
  Facts section for exactly how to talk about it. Everything else stays
  strictly Wisconsin.
- **Hours cap what is possible.** Sunday closes around 7pm, so a Sunday
  concept has to work in daylight. Monday through Thursday the doors do not
  open until 4pm.
- **The property is the asset.** 35 acres, 18-hole disc golf course, 3.5
  miles of trails. Ideas that use the outdoors beat ideas that could run in
  any bar.
- **Small kitchen, simple menu.** Tacos, quesadillas, nachos, hot dogs,
  pizza. Do not propose anything requiring a real kitchen expansion without
  flagging the cost.
- **Breakfast is discontinued** (2026-07-26). Never propose a breakfast or
  brunch service.
- **Families and dogs are part of the identity.** Kids come to Bingo. Dogs
  are welcome on leash. Ideas do not have to be 21+.
- **Do not duplicate what already runs.** Before proposing an event, check the
  weekly table above and the monthly list in the parent skill.
