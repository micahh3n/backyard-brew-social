## Voice DNA — the pattern underneath all five

- **Short paragraphs, real blank lines between them.** Never a dense block.
  Usually 2-4 short paragraphs, sometimes as little as two sentences total
  (Tacos + Poker above is 27 words across two lines and is still a complete,
  real caption).
- **Openers are flat statements or facts, almost never questions.** "Cheers to
  2 years." "Karaoke tonight, 8pm start." "Tonight's tacos, straight off the
  grill." No caption in the real sample opens with "Get ready for" or a
  rhetorical question.
- **Membership mentions are specific and a little funny, never a fixed line.**
  "Course Members get unlimited disc golf if a lap sounds better than warming
  up your voice" is a joke built around that night's event, not a recited
  benefit.
- **Closers are short and often just logistics**, not a crafted pull-quote:
  "Open till 9 tonight." "Bring your friends, we're open till midnight (or
  later)." The anniversary post is the one exception — it closes by echoing
  its own opener ("Cheers to 2 years, and cheers to all of you"), which works
  because it's a milestone post, not the everyday move.
- **Emoji are sparse and specific** — one or two per post (🍻 for the bar
  itself, 🌕 for the actual full moon, 🌮 for the actual tacos), never
  stacked, never decorating every line.
- **Grammar is casual, not corporate, and not fussed over.** Two real, unedited
  typos sit in the sample above ("lets support" / "if your interested"). This
  doesn't mean deliberately writing worse — `caption-voice-rules.md`'s
  standard-spelling rule stays — but it means the bar is "sounds like a person
  actually typing this," not "reads like it went through a style guide."
  Casual and a little imperfect beats polished and generic every time.
- **A real, specific reason to act, stated plainly, not dressed up.** "Free to
  play with a beer purchase," "Spots are limited," "DM us" — the ask is
  always concrete and near the end, never buried in adjectives.
- **First person shows up on milestone posts** ("my family") — that's dad
  speaking as himself, not "the business." Reserve this for genuinely personal
  moments, not every post; using it everywhere would flatten what makes it
  land here.
- **"Appreciate ya" / "we appreciate ya" is a real recurring line** (corrected
  2026-08-23) — the one deliberate exception to standard grammar in
  `caption-voice-rules.md`. Use it where a genuine thank-you closer fits.
  Don't manufacture other dropped-ending slang from this — it's one specific
  habit, not a general permission.
- **A real running bit: the tap pours fill from the bottom of the cup up**,
  and Backyard Brew is the only bar in the area that has this — the bar
  already jokes about the pour itself ("don't poke your hole," "you'll get
  soaked," about the cup's bottom seal). Funny, a little cheeky on purpose,
  don't sand it down. Use as a bit when a tap/beer mention calls for it, not
  forced into every pour reference.
- **The magnet collectible is a bigger deal than the pour joke, not a
  footnote.** Each cup has a magnet on the bottom, collected after finishing
  the drink, building toward a full set for a prize. **Bingo Night has its
  own themed set — spell B-I-N-G-O with the magnets, get a free beer.** This
  is a real, specific, gamified hook that belongs as a headline detail in
  Bingo captions specifically, not buried after the prize-table tease. Check
  with Micah before assuming other nights have their own sets (only Bingo's
  is confirmed).

## What was still missing from the first sample batch (2026-08-23)

Micah's reaction to the first live test (Bingo Night samples): "sorta" sounds
like him, but two things were underweighted even though the rules already
call for them:

- **FOMO has to actually create urgency to walk in, not just exist as a
  rule that got technically satisfied.** A curiosity gap alone isn't FOMO —
  it needs a reason this specific night is worth not missing (a deadline, a
  cutoff, a "this week only" detail), stacked on top of the hook, not
  standing in for it.
- **The social hook has to be a real, explicit ask** — tag someone specific,
  not just an atmosphere that might get shared. If a caption doesn't name the
  mechanism (tag, comment, share), it doesn't have one, no matter how good
  the writing is otherwise.
- **The lesson generalizes: having a rule in `caption-voice-rules.md` isn't
  the same as a caption actually landing it.** Before showing a draft, check
  it does something concrete for both goals Micah stated directly — gets
  seen/shared (tag/comment/share hook, explicit), and gets someone in the
  door tonight (real urgency, not just a fact stated flatly). Both, every
  time, not one or the other.
- **Fifth round correction (still 2026-08-23): a dedicated vendor-recruitment
  post needs real event facts (dates, times, setup windows, what's
  happening), not just the bare "it's free, DM us" line.** This refines
  rather than overturns the poster skill's vendor-copy rule — the
  distinction is **event-appeal facts vendors want to know** (dates, times,
  the full-moon theme, hiking trails open, the new Sunday market's
  permanent noon-4pm slot) are fair game and were the actual gap, versus
  **application-process specifics** (screening criteria, a bring-your-own
  list, an email address) which stay out, per Micah's original 2026-08-19
  call. A dedicated recruitment post is also the one caption type allowed to
  run past the normal FB length cap — it's carrying real informational
  weight for a niche audience, not a routine scroll-past post.
- **Fourth round correction (still 2026-08-23): every Market & Brews-related
  post needs both the Sept 13 Sunday-move plug and a vendor/musician
  recruitment callout, not just some of them.** I'd only put the Sept 13 note
  on one or two posts and the vendor ask on none. Both belong on all three
  market posts in a cluster (teaser, day-of, tie-in) — just phrased
  differently each time, same as any other repeated fact across an event's
  three touches. **Vendor-facing copy stays short, per the poster skill's
  existing rule** (`backyard-brew-posters/references/likes-dislikes.md`):
  it's free, DM us, name musicians explicitly alongside vendors. No
  screening criteria, no setup times, no email address in the caption.
- **Third round correction (still 2026-08-23): the 2:30pm tie-in posts
  defaulted to restating the 11am post's own facts, just shorter**, instead of
  picking a genuinely different angle from `/sunday`'s tie-in table (recap,
  food spotlight, vibe candid, regulars/staff shoutout, tap wall). Having that
  table exist in the command file didn't stop the failure — it has to be
  actively checked against, not just present. **The test: read the 11am and
  2:30 post for the same day side by side. If the 2:30 could be produced by
  deleting sentences from the 11am post, it's wrong — it needs a different
  specific, not a shorter version of the same specific.**
- **Second round correction (still 2026-08-23): "doors close at 9" as the
  urgency line failed** because Bingo starts 6:30 and is over well before 9 —
  the bar's closing time has nothing to do with whether someone makes it to
  bingo. **Urgency has to be logically load-bearing for the actual event, not
  a default reach for whatever time exists.** Real urgency for a routine
  event usually comes from the event's own mechanics (a cutoff, a prize that
  requires being there, a limited seat count if that's true) rather than the
  bar's hours. Check that the "urgent" fact would actually change someone's
  behavior before using it.

## Weekly vendor/musician recruitment post (standing, added 2026-08-23)

`/sunday` now produces a 22nd post every week — Monday ~9am, dedicated to
growing the vendor/musician roster for Backyard Market & Brews. Full spec
lives in `/sunday` itself; this ledger just tracks its actual wording so it
doesn't repeat itself week to week (see the rolling log below — this post's
opener gets logged there every time, same as any event post).

**Rotate the lead angle week to week**, since it's the same ask every time:
- Week 1 (2026-08-25 batch): urgency/scarcity — limited Moonlight Market
  spots plus "get in from week one" on the new Sunday slot.
- Next real week: try the community-pride angle instead ("this is what
  supporting local actually looks like") rather than urgency again.
- The week after: try the "good beer, real crowd, room that listens" draw.
- Don't reach for urgency/scarcity two weeks running — it stops being FOMO
  and starts being noise if it's the only lever pulled.

## Hook technique library — named technique, real Backyard Brew example

Pulled from proven short-form social copywriting practice (curiosity gaps,
pattern interrupts, specificity) and the `marketing-skills:social` /
`ad-creative` / `marketing-psychology` skills already installed. **The
technique layers on top of the real voice above — it never replaces it.** A
technically-correct hook that drifts back into generic ad-copy voice is a
failure, not a win. Goal is always both: more views/interactions, and people
who see it actually showing up and knowing what this place is.

- **First line has to stand alone (Instagram truncates at ~125 characters).**
  The whole hook lives in line one, because that's all anyone sees before
  tapping "more." Real example above: "Cheers to 2 years. 🍻" — six words,
  complete idea, makes you want the rest. Weak version: burying the milestone
  two sentences in.
- **Specificity beats adjectives.** "Course Members get unlimited disc golf if
  a lap sounds better than warming up your voice" beats "members get great
  perks" every time — a real, concrete, slightly funny specific over a vague
  claim. Numbers, names, and exact details ("$5 day pass," "6:40pm cutoff,"
  "27 vendors this week" — only when the number is real) are more shareable
  and more believable than "a great time."
- **Pattern interrupt in the visual formatting, not just the words.** The
  Moonlight Market post opens with "‼️Mark it now: ‼️" — a formatting move
  that stops a scroll before the sentence even registers. Use sparingly — it
  only works because it's rare.
- **Curiosity gap, held honestly.** The prize-vault style hook ("we're not
  telling you what's in the prize table until you walk in") works because the
  payoff is real and arrives the same night. Never open a gap that doesn't
  pay off — that's the fastest way to train people to stop clicking.
- **The ask is concrete and near the end, not vague.** "DM us," "Spots are
  limited," "Free to play with a beer purchase" — a specific, low-friction
  action beats "let us know what you think" every time. This is the existing
  share-mechanism rule in `caption-voice-rules.md`, just named as the
  technique it actually is.
- **Save/share triggers: information density people want to reference later.**
  A post listing exact times, prices, and what's included (the Moonlight
  Market post) gets saved because someone needs to check it again before
  Thursday. A pure vibe post with no logistics doesn't get saved the same way
  — vibe posts earn shares/comments instead, logistics posts earn saves.
  Match the mechanism to the post type rather than chasing all three on every
  post.
